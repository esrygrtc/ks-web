%TAG%|gate-4-green|2026-07-29%BODY%gate-4 green: one command, an agent killed with its own daemon, back at work in 25 seconds

The demo the company was built to give. `ks demo --task fixture --kill-at 3m
--headless` boots a real Claude Code specimen on a vendored four-bug repo,
checkpoints it, then hard-kills BOTH the microVM and ksd itself — the daemon
that supervises it — restarts the daemon, and resumes. Measured downtime:
25.2 seconds, of which Firecracker's own snapshot/load was 4 milliseconds.
The specimen came back and fixed all four bugs.

29,871 tokens were not re-purchased: work already paid for when the kill
landed, served from the gateway's store instead of bought twice. That number
is MEASURED. The dollar figure beside it is labeled ESTIMATED, because the
rates are an assumption and the tokens are not.

Sabotage green: with checkpointing disabled the same kill is unsurvivable.

%TAG%|gate-5-green|2026-07-29%BODY%gate-5 green: three branches, one checkpoint, three histories that differ only by their steer

The MVP build plan is complete — gates 0, 1, 2a, 2b, 3, 4 and 5 all green.

Phase 5 solved the deferred child-egress dragon the honest way: a forked
guest keeps the parent's addressing and never learns it was cloned, while
per-child network namespaces let identical guest IPs coexist and reach the
gateway independently. Steering lives only in ksgw, injected into a branch's
next call and recorded with both pre- and post-steer body hashes — which is
how the gate proved three siblings differed by their steering and by nothing
else: base sha256 identical on all three, steered bodies all distinct.

The gate earned its keep. It caught an attribution bug that billed every
forked child's work to its parent, and two more besides. All three were
fixed and disclosed before the gate re-ran.

%TAG%|gate-6-green|2026-07-31%BODY%gate-6 green: durability measured as a rate, 25/25 with a Wilson interval that admits what 25 trials can carry

Three findings, none in the core claim. A connection leak that killed any
session past its ninth checkpoint and that 25 kill-cycles structurally could
not see — the soak exists because kill-cycles reset the very counters that
long-lived sessions accumulate. A preservation breach where our own cleanup
destroyed the artifact a ruling had protected. And a gate that could not read
its own evidence while the system was healthy: six false alarms and one false
comfort from a single parser defect.

The fix for the third is a mechanism, not a promise: ks ledger, production Go
code sharing the writers' types, now the only path a gate may read evidence
through.

82/82 scheduled checkpoints, one witness nonce strictly monotonic, zero
cadence gaps, 19.1x dedup, RSS peak growth +0 MiB on both daemons. A
14.5-hour-old checkpoint woken live in 14.3 s reading COUNT=63 while its
living twin had reached 4841.

%TAG%|v0.1.0-mvp|2026-07-31%BODY%KeepState v0.1.0-mvp — the durable agent session cloud

An MVP that runs AI agents inside Firecracker microVMs and gives them five
verbs: run, checkpoint, fork, resume, replay. A live agent is killed mid-task
— the microVM and the daemon supervising it — and is working again seconds
later, same session, same memory, mid-sentence.

Frozen at nine green gates. The standing invariant is that no claim outlives
its gate and no claim precedes one; docs/claims.md is the promise surface and
gate 7 machine-checks that every row in it cites a tag that exists.
